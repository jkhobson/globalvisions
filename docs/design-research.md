# Design research: what looks right to 12 and 13 year olds

Notes behind the light theme, gathered November 2026. Please check the sources yourself before relying on them.

## What the sources say

- **Teens reject "babyish" design.** The Nielsen Norman Group says teens dislike being grouped with young children ("kid" is a teen repellent). Neutral graphics work better than juvenile ones. Heavy animation and bright, clashing colour schemes suit younger children, not teens. Teens value good looks but dislike clutter and pointless effects. Source: [Teenagers on the web, NN/g, 2019](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/usability-of-websites-for-teenagers/).
- **Short text, big type, touch-friendly.** In the same study, teens read less willingly than adults. They do best with short chunks, white space and bullet points, and they dislike tiny fonts. Small tap targets, drag and drop, and hover effects cause problems on phones. Many teens use only a phone.
- **Warm, direct and not patronising.** UK Department for Education research with young people found they like colour, images and interactive tools. They dislike clutter, childish gimmicks, long explanations, and being shown too many choices at once. Source: [Designing for young people, DfE design histories](https://design-histories.education.gov.uk/find-a/designing-for-young-people).
- **Playful but simple shapes.** Duolingo describes its look as bold, bright and rounded: vibrant colours on white, simple shapes and exaggerated characters. The aim is to make mistakes feel less scary. Source: [Shape language: Duolingo's art style](https://blog.duolingo.com/shape-language-duolingos-art-style/).
- **Collect and play.** Classroom games popular with middle schoolers, such as Blooket, lean on collectable characters and many small game modes. Kahoot keeps a plain, fast race with a live leaderboard. Source: [Blooket vs Kahoot for middle school (Screenwise)](https://screenwiseapp.com/guides/blooket-vs-kahoot-for-middle-school-learning). This is a review site, not research.
- **Light versus dark.** I did not find solid research showing that teens prefer one or the other. Duolingo, for example, is built on white backgrounds. I have not checked every app, so treat "most learning apps are light" as a guess. That is why the app now has a switch.

## What this means for Perspecta

1. A **bright light theme** with white space, plus the dark theme. Each student picks Auto (follows their phone), Light or Dark on the Me page.
2. Use **chunky, pressable buttons and answer cards**: rounded corners with a darker bottom edge, so they feel like game buttons and are easy to tap.
3. Use **a few bold accent colours** (blue, gold, green, purple) on white. Avoid rainbow clutter.
4. Use **friendly but not childish** language and art. Keep the "agent" and "mission" framing, which reads as cool rather than cute.
5. Ideas for later:
   - A **mascot or collectable** layer, such as stamps or agent badges, which Blooket's success suggests is sticky.
   - **Skill colours**, one colour per GP skill.
